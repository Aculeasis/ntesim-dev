# Product requirements

Detailed interface and model reference for this [capability](spec.md). Requirements and scenarios live in that spec; this reference retains formats, bounds and technical rationale.

Build an open-source NTE simulator to accurately calculate and compare the DPS of different teams using different builds and rotations. The product goal is reproducible team DPS comparison under a declared combat scenario. Configure legal teams, execute authored rotations, compare damage and explain its causes. One Rust engine serves native CLI and the existing React/TypeScript browser studio through WASM. Accuracy is conditional on the declared scenario and checked model; uncertainty and approximations remain visible.

The native CLI and browser Studio use the same Rust engine. [Script inspection](../script-inspection/reference.md) supplies structured ID-based party stats/resources and source diagnostics to both consumers. [Development](../../../docs/DEVELOPMENT.md#choosing-checks) selects checks by the affected boundary.

## DPS relevance and modeling boundary

Required fidelity follows the causal path to team damage: packet amounts/counts/targets, buffs/debuffs, reactions, resources, action availability, rotation timing and the DPS denominator. A mechanic need not deal damage itself to matter. Healing can trigger a damage buff or change an HP-dependent coefficient; shields can enable weapon bonuses; incoming damage, movement or interruption can change available attacks and hit coverage.

Mechanics without a direct or indirect DPS effect may still be implemented fully when easy and cheap. If they materially increase implementation complexity or runtime cost, simplify or omit them. Full survival, movement, enemy AI, visual effects and city simulation are not completion requirements. Preserve any damage-relevant triggers, thresholds, recipients, lifetimes and state transitions; a minimal event/state model may replace unrelated detail.

Record each deliberate simplification/omission in the existing owning contract/receipt, with what changes, why it cannot affect the declared DPS scenario, applicability, and the condition requiring a fuller model. Carry applicable assumptions/limitations into results and exports. For example, skipping HP restoration is valid for a healing-triggered buff only if heal amounts, effective healing, overheal and HP cannot change that scenario's damage dependencies. Do not generalize that choice to HP-dependent teams.

Combat targets are one or more stationary training dummies with declared combat stats. All eligible dummies are in range and inside attack areas by scenario definition. An AoE with no recipient cap hits every eligible dummy; an attack capped at K recipients hits at most K eligible dummies, leaving the others undamaged by that hit. Single-target attacks retain one recipient. Preserve owner-specific target selection, per-hit recipient caps, hit counts, damage distribution, target-count conditions and damage-relevant delivery timing; all dummies being in range does not waive those rules. Unknown caps or selection/distribution rules remain unresolved mechanics.

Spatial coordinates, native AoE radii/shapes, placement and movement simulation are outside this product's target model and are not missing implementation data. All-in-range coverage is a declared scenario rule, not a hypothesis about native radii. Damage-relevant proximity/range conditions use explicit scenario conditions where needed, without reconstructing geometry. Results describe DPS against these dummies, not hit coverage in a spatial encounter. Existing spatial fields/fallbacks require migration to this contract; the scope change alone does not change executable behavior or runtime readiness.

Unknown relevance is an unresolved dependency, not permission to set behavior to zero. Unsupported reached damage dependencies still prevent complete results. Documented DPS-irrelevant omissions do not block baseline completion; a simplification that changes DPS is a visible model hypothesis requiring independent causal/numerical checks. Prefer existing cheap shared services over another approximation layer. This policy defines desired scope; it does not change runtime flags or existing admission rules without implementation and checks.

## Rolling data policy

The product follows rolling releases using current available data, not a frozen Global date, region or patch. Research may use local client files, public descriptions, combat recordings, spreadsheets and other checkable sources. Prefer current, relevant evidence; an unavailable exact build, region or retrieval date does not block investigation or a supported finding. Record a source link or local file/field reference and useful date/version information when readily available. Per-source hashes, immutable acquisition packages, exact hotfix certification and historical replay are not current research or release requirements.

New supported findings may supersede older mechanics in their owning card. Keep material conflicts and uncertainty visible; recency alone does not resolve conflicting rules, and missing data does not authorize invention. Historical source bytes and receipts need not be rewritten or renamed. Existing runtime package IDs, hash checks and saved fixtures describe the current implementation; they do not restrict new research. Multi-version support and stronger archival provenance are deferred until the simulator is useful. Do not build migration infrastructure for them now.

## Scope and priority

- Content target: currently released characters and combat equipment using the rolling data policy. The existing 25-character dataset is a starting inventory, not a permanent roster or cutoff.
- Legal progression, purchased/effective skill levels, awakening effect choices, weapons/refinements, cartridge sets and additive module/substats via `add stats` (without automatic specialization or module geometry), initial HP/owner resources and shared party Fons, targets and time/victory stop policies. Fons is distinct from Chiz's combat Grains/Grain Loans; reached consumers read the same explicit balance.
- Ordinary chains/holds in seconds, next-action cancellation windows, switches during casts where permitted, evades/counters, automatic available Support Skills on switching, forms/recasts, summons/off-field attacks, reactions/cycles/energy, break and buffs/debuffs. Healing, shields, incoming damage and other support mechanics follow the DPS relevance policy. Distinguish field control, actor recovery and pending hits; switching need not cancel an ongoing tap/hold/channel. Each owner's continuation/cancel/refund rules are checked separately. Plunge and explicit jump follow required damage-relevant legality/timing; difficult cancels need separate support.
- Human-readable sequential combat rotation scripts with exact numeric variables/constants, live readiness/owner-resource/state/modifier queries, conditions and loops; typed boolean action flags, no user functions, priority lists or game controls. Enemy scripting/movement is deferred. An ordinary input that fails cooldown, energy, recovery or state admission stops the run immediately with a source-bound diagnostic. Waiting and conditional readiness checks are explicit authored inputs. One real elapsed combat-time axis owns public waits/horizons/reports and the DPS denominator, independent of host runtime; Hotori Time Stop is an owner state with timer/resource/combo and process-specific progress/pause rules. Deterministic seeds, continuing cycles, bounded search and comparisons remain required.
- Explicit modeled scenario inputs include periodic/single delivered damage and boolean assumptions of successful Critical Dodge/parry for dummy targets. They reuse owner transitions and preserve ordinary costs/timing/effects plus source/scenario provenance; no enemy AI or arbitrary damage/proc replacement is required. Executor/report diagnostics identify prolonged idle, explicit waits and lost real combat time, distinguishing waits/exhausted scripts from failed admission and active casts/recovery/channels; warning policy is outside DSL syntax.
- Traceable reports/exports with engine/data/input identities, seeds, assumptions, unsupported behavior and failures. Static browser app, typed Worker/WASM boundaries, local persistence/import/export and offline caching; no server simulation/accounts/database or historical storage migrations.

## Validation and completion

Completion means a useful, reproducible, independently checked combat model. Community damage spreadsheets, independent hand calculations and consistent combat logic validate supported behavior; static client tables/graphs and public recordings may refine it. The [modeling policy](../../../docs/DEVELOPMENT.md) owns hypotheses, strict/model execution, unsupported dependencies and investigation limits.

The damage-relevant scope above remains the backlog after the first usable baseline; justified documented irrelevant omissions need not become future implementation cards. These routine modeling choices need no separate human approval under the working rules. Completion requires tested legal configurations/rotations, CLI/browser parity, reports, practical reference checks and a reproducible distributable release. Current capabilities and API flags are reported in [progress](../../README.md); changing requirements does not change runtime readiness.

## First usable baseline

This is the minimum combat boundary for the first release, not a claim that every kit branch is complete:

- **Characters:** all 25 released identities have at least one legal endgame A0 build with unlocked damage-relevant ordinary passives, a full ordinary attack chain, normal skill and ultimate. Include resulting damage-relevant form/recast transitions, delayed/periodic/off-field packets, summons, resources and team effects. Reached healing/shields and other support mechanics follow the DPS relevance policy. A damage-relevant core form or passive cannot be deferred merely because its implementation is complex.
- **Equipment/builds:** every verified released weapon/set has a legal equipped configuration with actual static stats and damage-relevant unconditional/conditional effects, with trigger and expiry checks. Candidate table presence does not verify release; known released items with missing damage dependencies remain pending. Baseline examples specify progression, purchased/effective skill levels, awakening and refinement. At least one legal refinement is checked per weapon; every advertised additional progression, awakening choice or refinement executes its selected/unlocked damage dependencies and declares any irrelevant omissions. Unimplemented damage-relevant selections are rejected rather than accepted with partial stats/effects.
- **Continuing combat:** each character participates in a saved ordinary rotation covering attacks, skill and ultimate in at least two continuing cycles, with legal waits/energy generation as needed. Preserve resources, buffs, cooldowns, targets, clocks, pending effects and RNG across cycles. Shared multi-character and single/multi-target cases exercise recipients, switching, off-field delivery and required reactions/break; initialization or resetting a fixture cannot demonstrate repeatability. No fixed duration or optimal-team requirement.
- **User workflow and checks:** legal build/rotation input, live-state execution, causal reports and comparisons work through the native CLI and browser using the same engine using the same engine. Each owner has independent Rust numerical/logic checks and causal boundary tests; final native/WASM/Worker outputs and diagnostics agree. Native development retains bounded API smoke checks at contract changes and integration milestones. Saved inputs bind identities, assumptions and seeds. Failed, truncated or unsupported runs are excluded from successful aggregates/rankings.

Additional damage-relevant holds/counters/evades, difficult cancels, awakening/refinement selections or other branches may remain pending when outside the declared baseline configurations and neither requested nor automatically reached. If a baseline action or equipped-effect case depends on one, its checked model is required. Requesting or reaching an unsupported damage dependency produces a precise diagnostic and prevents complete results; irrelevant omissions follow the policy above. Supported comparisons retain assumptions; a reached unresolved damage-relevant passive/proc cannot be made optional by omitting its command or turning off its handler. Enemy scripting/movement remains deferred; explicit minimal scenario inputs may supply required DPS triggers.

Record supported selections and remaining branches in existing owner contracts/receipts/tests and expose them through diagnostics; do not create a second coverage registry. Baseline readiness requires the mandatory boundary for every released owner, while full-kit/full-domain claims require the remaining plan work. [Release requirements](../distribution/reference.md) add packaging, performance, accessibility and distribution checks.

After usable combat coverage, select strong teams, tune rotations and provide ready configs and optional comparative tier-list examples under the working rules. Original code uses MIT; third-party distribution rights are reviewed separately.
